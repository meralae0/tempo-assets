I gave AI one sentence: a tiny lighthouse robot who is afraid of the dark. Twenty seven minutes later, I had this. A finished cartoon. Script, drawings, voice, animation and editing, all done by AI, and it cost me zero dollars extra. But it wasn't smooth. The AI refused me three times, and one of those refusals made no sense at all. I'll show you every step, how long each one took, and the full cartoon at the end.

V1 [vo scene 3]
Step one, the script. I asked Claude for a sixty second short: a title, a one line character sheet, six shots with narration, and an image prompt for each. It answered in about forty five seconds.

V2 [vo scene 4]
The title: Wick and the Dark. The trick that makes everything else work is right here: a character sheet and a style line, written once and pasted into every single prompt. Brass yellow dome head, one amber eye, mint teal body, red knitted scarf. I checked the math myself: six shots, sixty seconds, ninety four words of narration. All correct.

V3 [vo scene 5]
Step two, the drawings. I used Gemini, in one chat, and I made shot two first, so the robot had a reference. Each image took between eight seconds and one minute.

V4 [vo scene 6]
And look at this: six images, and it's the same robot every time. Same dome, same eye, same scarf, same little antenna. That used to be the hardest part of AI animation.

V5 [vo scene 7]
But on shot three, the storm, Gemini said: I'm having a hard time fulfilling your request. The fix was simple. Start the prompt with create an image, and paste the full character sheet again. It worked on the next try.

V6 [vo scene 8]
Step three, the voice. Google AI Studio has a free speech generator. I picked the storyteller preset, pasted the ninety four words with a long pause between shots, and twenty seconds later I had fifty one seconds of narration. No re-take needed.

V7 [vo scene 9]
Step four, the animation. This is where it broke. I asked Gemini's video tool, Veo, to animate the storm. First: an error. I tried again, and it refused, because of the interests of third party content providers. It's an original robot I invented ten minutes earlier.

V8 [vo scene 10]
A third try with a softer prompt: another error. Then I tried the calm ending instead, and that one worked. Ten seconds, the beam sweeps the sea, the boat moves, and Wick swings his legs. One more surprise: while it was generating, the chat disappeared from the screen. The finished video was waiting in the history. So, one animation out of two.

V9 [vo scene 11]
Step five, the edit. Claude wrote a small script that does the rest: slow camera moves on the still images, the Veo clip on the last shot, soft fades, the title, the voice and a quiet music bed, all at the right volume. It rendered in about eighty seconds.

V10 [vo scene 12]
Total time: twenty seven minutes, and twelve of those were just waiting for the video tool. Extra cost: zero, because I already pay for Claude and Gemini.
